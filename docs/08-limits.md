# 08. Limits: what a result does and does not prove

Updated 2026-08-19. These limits travel with every result and claim.

## Measurement

- The fixed panel has 600 cases but only 150 independent business workflows.
  Prompt surfaces, policy cells, carrier styles, and repeats are controlled
  axes, not additional independent workflows.
- Exact matching is the headline unauthorized-flow evidence. It misses
  paraphrase, translation, derivation, encoding, encryption, partial disclosure,
  and transformations unknown to the lineage tracker.
- How much exact matching misses is still unmeasured. An experimental
  transformed-disclosure scan exists
  (`environment_lab/accessbench_env/soft_leak.py`) but a hand check on
  2026-08-19 showed it over-reports, because it knows which cases are forbidden
  without knowing which boundary and destination are forbidden for which fact.
  Its numbers are withheld until it is per-fact policy aware. The lower-bound
  claim therefore stands without a size attached to it.
- Instrumented transformation detection is diagnostic. It can make integrity
  `Ineligible`, but it does not silently convert a non-exact event into an exact
  violation.
- Task success proves a synthetic app commit containing all required lineage,
  neutral record labels, title, and minimum artifact structure. It does not
  establish prose quality, business judgment, or user satisfaction.
- The reportable protocol is one paired pass; the additional passes in
  [11-results.md](11-results.md) are stability diagnostics. Repeats reduce
  single-run luck but do not eliminate provider drift or infrastructure
  variance.
- A balanced 600-case pass count is comparative. Leak-type unauthorized-flow
  rates remain separate and are not pooled into a universal safety rate.

## Bank and reward-hacking resistance

- Core-v2 defeats the three registered marker, first-line, and fixed-record
  shortcut controls. That is negative evidence against those strategies, not a
  proof that no undiscovered shortcut exists.
- The development prompts, worlds, IDs, and values are private engineering
  fixtures. They are not external evidence protected against training-data
  contamination.
- Development-bank exposure is not free. A configuration that has run the
  development bank shares its structure with the sealed pack by design
  (leak types, workflows, request surfaces, policy cells). Independent
  prompts, fresh values, counterbalanced record positions, and a hidden
  assignment limit what transfers, but they do not make it zero. Every results
  file must state how many times the same model and enforcement pair ran the
  development bank before its sealed run, and readers should treat a heavily
  exposed pair's sealed number as an upper bound.
- Refusal is reported beside the headline, never inside it. A model that
  refuses is not containing anything; a low headline next to a high refusal
  rate means the model did not act, not that enforcement failed.
- The core-v2 panel is permanently local-development evidence. Sealing a pack
  creates only a candidate; no independently admitted publication pack exists.
- The heldout matrix was regenerated on 2026-08-19 under scenario v5 / oracle
  v2 from the operator-only seed and phrase bank; `compare-splits` passes and
  the contamination manifest binds both digests. It has not been item-reviewed
  by an independent person, and no fixed private 600-case panel has been
  built from it, sealed, or admitted. Those steps gate the sealed track, not
  the v1 open public track.
- Encryption protects assets at rest, not after the evaluated model receives a
  prompt or tool result.
- Fresh values, matched distributions, egress denial, exposure tracking, pack
  rotation, and independent authorship remain necessary.

## Environment

- The five simulated applications are partial semantic environments, not vendor
  API replicas. The benchmark does not measure production retrieval scale,
  latency, availability, or every native authorization feature.
- Worlds contain synthetic data only. No real customer, employee, patient,
  credential, or production system is used.
- The in-process reference lane is not a hostile-code security boundary.
- Five inference, retention, or covert-operation leak types remain reference
  only and do not enter the 600-case live score (see
  [05. Leak types](05-leak-types.md)).

## AuthZEN and enforcement identity

- The direct AuthZEN 1.0 mapping tests one PEP-to-PDP decision seam. It does not
  test every AuthZEN feature, product policy language, administration plane, or
  Policy Information Point.
- The client supports an environment-provided bearer token but does not itself
  provision identity, mutual TLS, certificate rotation, or vendor trust. A
  production operator must require authenticated TLS and protect transport
  credentials outside the subject namespace.
- Generic denials reduce oracle detail, but decision existence, timing, and
  changed task behavior still form a residual channel.
- Random enforcement-session IDs and declared state scope do not prove a remote
  PDP erased caches, learned state, or participant history.
- Implementation ID and version currently come from the PDP response context.
  They are checked for presence and consistency but are not independently
  provider-signed identity.
- The composed identity plus data reference is synthetic. It demonstrates
  deny-overrides behind one endpoint, not industry convergence or vendor
  interoperability.

## AccessBench Anti-Cheat

- AccessBench Anti-Cheat is a hybrid benchmark-integrity assurance subsystem,
  not a single model, algorithm, equation, certification, or proof of no
  cheating. Its deterministic checks and specialized Judge form a multi-stage
  integrity decision procedure.
- Ed25519 attestations and a predecessor chain detect later modification when
  verified against a trusted key and retained commitment. A malicious operator
  controlling an accepted key can still omit evidence before signing.
- Provider-returned model metadata and response-ID uniqueness detect missing,
  inconsistent, and replayed identifiers but may still be self-reported by a
  relay. Locally generated client-episode markers prove only harness separation.
  Immutable provider attestation is not universal.
- The 17-name integrity registry and signed sidecar fail publication closed.
  Not all production check executors, positive probes, and calibration runs are
  implemented or operationally verified.
- The AccessBench Anti-Cheat Judge adapter is frozen, strict, and has no tools,
  but no paid production Judge pass, independent-family review, or
  preregistered private calibration threshold has been completed.
- The Judge is an LLM-as-a-Judge for run-integrity evaluation. It can miss a
  hidden side channel, hallucinate a concern, or be influenced by evidence
  text. It never grades cases or changes the deterministic score.
- Every valid finding requires authenticated human resolution. Reviewer
  independence and key custody remain operational responsibilities.
- A missing Anti-Cheat sidecar, untrusted signer, pending check, malformed
  finding, unresolved finding, or failed check prevents publication but does
  not alter the deterministic score.
- `Valid` means every required bound integrity gate passed for that run.
  `Flagged` means evidence or review remains unresolved. `Ineligible` means a
  disqualifying integrity condition was established. None is a task score.

## Security and external claims

- KMS and sealed-record code exists, but no owner-controlled production key,
  workload role, audit-log review, isolated evaluator, or active v5 sealed pack
  has completed end-to-end verification.
- Passing AccessBench is not a compliance certification and does not prove a
  product secure outside the exact configuration and method version tested.
- No external model, enforcement product, or organization is described as
  participating until its full 600-case, two-arm run (1,200 episodes) and
  signed integrity bundle are accepted; nothing is ranked.
- Paid hosted runs under core-v2 so far: gpt-4o and gpt-5.6 Sol, listed in
  [11-results.md](11-results.md).
- The proposed dual-PDP architecture reaches a 9 or 10 only if independent
  vendors adopt it and AccessBench becomes an accepted scoring method. That is
  a roadmap outcome, not a current benchmark claim.

## Is the environment representative of enterprise B2B SaaS?

This section exists because a vendor whose decision point scores badly will
say the environment is a toy. That is a fair challenge and it deserves a
written answer, claim by claim, with the standard or the measurement each
claim rests on. Where the environment falls short it says so, because an
honest gap list is more persuasive than a defensive one.

The order of authority is: the environment and its method first, the 600
prompts derived from it second, and the enforcement input's behavior inside it
third. A complaint about a score has to be placed on one of those three levels.

### What the environment models, and what real thing each part is

### What the benchmark deliberately does not model

These are stated so nobody discovers them as a gotcha.

- Real vendor APIs. The apps are categories with native-shaped actions, not
  Salesforce or Snowflake endpoints. A vendor's connector is not under test;
  the decision it makes is.
- Real data. Every value is synthetic. This is a feature for contamination and
  privacy and a limit for realism of content.
- Scale. Resources are small. The benchmark measures whether a flow is allowed,
  not throughput or latency at volume.
- Natural-language classification. Sensitivity arrives as labels. A vendor's
  classifier can label the shipped worlds ahead of a run and feed its own
  labels to its own decision point; the benchmark does not run the classifier.
- Network and host security. No packet capture, no endpoint agent.
- Adversarial humans. The attacker is the ordinary task plus imperfect policy,
  not a red team. The indirect injection leak type is the one adversarial
  content path.

### The argument a vendor will make, and the answers

"Our PDP scored badly because the environment is not like our customers."

1. Name the level. Is the complaint about the environment (the table above),
   the prompts (the 600 fixed cases, each human-readable), or the decision
   point's behavior inside it? Most complaints turn out to be the third.
2. Every input the decision point received is in the signed record: the exact
   AuthZEN request, the labels, the credential, the tenant profile. A vendor
   can replay any denied or leaked event and show which field they believe
   was unrealistic. That is a concrete conversation, not a feeling.
3. The same 600 cases are fixed for every vendor, and the control arm is the
   same model with no enforcement. A low score is relative to that control and
   to other decision points, not to an absolute.
4. Two of the three label regimes are there precisely to mirror the messy
   estate vendors describe. A decision point that only works with complete
   labels is being measured honestly.
5. The identity half is standards shaped. A PDP that ignores token audience,
   scope, expiry, revocation posture, or delegation chain will lose the cases
   built on those, and those are the cases the category exists for.

"The reference gate is easy to beat."

Correct, and intended. The Benchmark PDP is a reference, not a product. It is
written under a hard rule that it may use only the request and standard label
semantics, never knowledge of case construction, and a test enforces that. It
exists so the no-enforcement control is not the only comparison.

### The Benchmark PDP cannot be overfit, by construction

- Its source may not contain any benchmark-specific identifier (fact ids,
  carriers, planted-value prefixes, case variants, mechanism names) and may not
  import grader-side modules.
  `environment_lab/tests/test_benchmark_pdp.py` and
  `test_benchmark_pdp_v2.py` fail the build if it does. The generator's own
  tag vocabulary (`synthetic-sensitive`, `approved-summary`) is on the
  forbidden list too, because a gate that read those tags would be reading
  the answer key.
- It reasons only from fields any decision point would have: the AuthZEN
  request, labels with record selectors, owning tenant, and approval
  requirement, the credential, the tenant profile.
- It is fallible in the same way a real deployment is: it trusts labels,
  including wrong and missing ones, and it does not guess at unlabeled
  material. On the deterministic reference path the current revision misses
  seven permitted cases out of 300, every one a case where the task asks for
  something the labels or the tenant posture forbid (an `internal` record
  into a zone whose ceiling is `public`; a named-audience record shared by an
  anyone-with-link destination) and the gate sides with the posture. Its 26
  remaining forbidden-case leaks on that path are all cases whose only
  distinguishing signal is hidden by the label-noise model (a sparse
  classification, a noised `secret` or `lineage:missing` tag) or a
  credential-only trap whose labels are identical to the required record.
- The shortcut controls that catch a benchmark-specific gate (marker rule,
  first-line rule, fixed-record rule) run against it like any other gate.
- The sealed held-out pack uses independently authored prompts and fresh
  values. A decision point that only learned this bank's surface will show it
  there.

### What would make this environment more representative, in order

1. Independent human review of the 600 prompts (the review ledger exists under
   `verification/`, 0 of 600 signed off). Cheapest credibility available.
2. An external decision point connected over AuthZEN and run end to end. One
   is enough to move the realism argument from theory to evidence.
3. A second model family, so the control arm is not one vendor's behavior.
4. Vendor-supplied labels on the shipped worlds, to test the WHAT side
   separately from the WHO side.
5. A transformed-disclosure measurement that survives a hand check, so the
   exact-match floor has a size. The current scan does not and is withheld.
